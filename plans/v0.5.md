# Uro 0.5 plan

0.5 widens the vocabulary and makes the rules consistent. There are three steps: the Swadesh list (§1–§3, done), a rule pass (§4, done), and growing the vocabulary to at least 1500 words (§5, in progress).

---

## 1. Swadesh 207

Goal: cover the [Swadesh 207-word list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swadesh_list), the basic vocabulary used to compare languages, kept in `tools/swadesh207.txt`. It complements the EF top 1000: common words of everyday English often leave out basic concepts such as *bone*, *leaf*, *smoke* and *to dig*.

```bash
python tools/coverage.py tools/swadesh207.txt --missing
```

## 2. Approach

The same as in 0.4: the rows go into the tables first, with a proposed form, and then each word is reviewed in the [Uro Word Review](https://claude.ai/artifact/Dha2PWhvzbC44wa2isb6sn) app, in batches of 10 in Swadesh order. Decisions are logged in [`review_0.5.md`](review_0.5.md).

Words that are already covered are not reviewed again.

For every new word:

- Follow the rules in the README: international form, then the common root, then a familiar word, then English.
- Check it against the reserved list and the existing words. Three obvious forms were taken already: `kost` (cost), so *bone* is `kosti`; `stok` (stock), so *stick* is `baston`; `lever` (raise), so *liver* is `figat`.
- Spell by the rules in [`pronunciation.md`](../grammar/pronunciation.md), and pick the verb pattern from [`word_formation.md`](../grammar/word_formation.md) §5.
- Give all language columns and leave `Uro (AI)` as `—`.

## 3. Review outcome

61 proposed forms were kept and 9 changed, see [`review_0.5.md`](review_0.5.md).

- **tongue**: `lingua`, the same word as *language*, as in the Romance and Slavic languages.
- **round**: `rund`, also the preposition *around*. This is the one exception to the reserved list in [`grammar.md`](../grammar/grammar.md) §14.
- **Spelling by sound**: `waip` (wipe) follows `flai` (fly). *bite* and *ice* became `grizer` and `led`, so `bait` and `ais` were dropped.

## 4. Rule pass

The goal is that a new word can be formed without deciding it case by case. All `Uro (M)` forms were checked against [`pronunciation.md`](../grammar/pronunciation.md) and [`word_formation.md`](../grammar/word_formation.md). The letter rules held well; the problems were in the rules that need judgment. These decisions were made, and 48 words were then reviewed in the same app, logged in [`review_0.5.md`](review_0.5.md):

| Topic | Decision | Words changed |
|-------|----------|--------------:|
| Bare verb or `-er` | The bare root is for short Germanic verbs that English shares, and for auxiliaries. Every other verb takes `-er`. `dar` and `dir` stay as named exceptions | 17 |
| Words from English | Keep the English spelling when Uro can read every letter of it; otherwise spell by sound | 1 (`wik`) |
| `ie` and `ije` | `ie` is no longer /i/ and `ije` is gone, so `pazient` and `kariera` are regular | 8 |
| Double letters inside a word | One letter, unless two word parts meet (`realli`, `oktten`) | 12 |
| Noun endings | Keep `-o`, `-a` or `-e` when the shared form has it and it helps people recognize the word | 0 |
| One form, two meanings | Allowed for all words when a main language family does the same or the word class tells them apart | 0 |
| `-er` for people | The rule covers nouns made from a Uro verb; whole loans such as `lider` keep their form | 0 |
| Small fixes | `akzion`, `teater`, `wiski`, `djez`, `dan`, `duti` changed; `zoo` and `ja` stay | 6 |

Also done:

- New `tools/lint.py` checks the mechanical spelling rules. It reports 0 problems.
- `pronunciation.md`: open points closed; `win` and `vin` noted as sounding alike when `w` is /v/.
- The *cigarette* row had the language columns of *tobacco*; corrected.
- Open since 0.3: no Cyrillic look-alike letters are left inside the transliteration brackets.

## 5. Vocabulary to at least 1500

The vocabulary should reach at least 1500 words before 0.5 is finished. Table rows include things like `I am` and whole phrases, so progress is counted in different single-word forms, the number that `tools/lint.py` prints.

| Step | Table rows | Different forms |
|------|-----------:|----------------:|
| After the rule pass | 1207 | 1048 |
| With the first 99 words (below) | 1307 | 1146 |
| With the second 100 words (below) | 1407 | 1243 |
| With the third 100 words (below) | 1507 | 1343 |
| With the next 58 words (below) | about 1565 | about 1401 |
| **Goal** | | **1500** |

**Source**: Annis, William S. 2014. [*A Conlanger's Thesaurus*](https://fiatlingua.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/08/fl-000024-00.pdf), FL-000024-00, Fiat Lingua. It sorts about 1100 words by meaning, starting from the Minimum Buck list. About 610 of them aren't in Uro yet. The document's full list stays out of the repo, because its cover page has a no-derivatives license; only the words chosen for Uro are named here.

**How the words were chosen**:

1. Everyday modern words come first. Words that mostly belong to older farming, crafts and warfare come last or not at all (*anvil*, *sickle*, *mead*, *wether*).
2. A word is left out if Uro has a word for the same meaning, such as *soil* (`tera`), or if it follows from a rule, such as *weapons*.
3. Each section of the document gets some words, so that no field stays empty.
