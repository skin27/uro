# De Kral i de Beger

A short tale, *The King and the Beggar*, written for Uro. Every word comes from the vocabulary tables, and every sentence follows [`grammar.md`](../grammar/grammar.md), using the default forms.

## Uro

Pre mult anos, un kral i su kraliza haf viver in un alto tower bi de kosta.
De kral hafed mult gold i silver, ale on not ared feli.
Un matin, un beger haf kom a de portal.
On hafed nul botas, i su pantalon ared smuts.
De kral haf kricher: "Ki ar ju? Go weg, o mi soldats wil kapturer ju!"
De beger not haf fuger. On haf shepter: "Je haf hunger i durst. Ju kan dar un poko pan a me?"
De kraliza hafed pieta, i ona haf dar supa, pan i un warm mantel a on.
Post de lunch, de beger haf sing un song, i al ludi haf rirer.
De kral haf demander: "Pro ke ar ju so feli, mem if ju haf nulting?"
De beger haf responder: "Richiti not kan kuper mir. Ju haf un tower, ale je haf de nebo over mi kop."
De kral haf penser longli. Dan on haf dar un gold moneta a de beger i haf dir: "Rester hir i instruer me."
Ale de beger haf smil, haf leger de moneta op de tabel, i haf walk a de port.
Od dan, kada vecher, de kral haf leger un kandel in de okno, pro un gast ke nikdi haf kom od novo.

## English

Many years ago, a king and his queen lived in a high tower by the shore.
The king had much gold and silver, but he was not happy.
One morning, a beggar came to the gate.
He had no shoes, and his trousers were dirty.
The king shouted: "Who are you? Go away, or my soldiers will catch you!"
The beggar did not flee. He whispered: "I am hungry and thirsty. Can you give me a little bread?"
The queen felt pity, and she gave him soup, bread and a warm coat.
After lunch, the beggar sang a song, and everyone laughed.
The king asked: "Why are you so happy, even though you have nothing?"
The beggar answered: "Wealth cannot buy peace. You have a tower, but I have the sky over my head."
The king thought for a long time. Then he gave a gold coin to the beggar and said: "Stay here and teach me."
But the beggar smiled, put the coin on the table, and walked to the harbor.
From then on, every evening, the king put a candle in the window, for a guest who never came again.

## Grammar notes

| Uro | Rule |
|-----|------|
| `Pre mult anos` | `pre` + time means *ago* |
| `haf viver`, `haf kom`, `haf kricher` | past tense, default (`haf` + verb) |
| `hafed`, `ared` | the short past of `haf` and `ar` |
| `not ared`, `not haf fuger` | `not` goes directly before the verb |
| `nul botas`, `nulting`, `nikdi` | negative words replace `not` |
| `Ki ar ju?`, `Pro ke ar ju so feli?` | inversion after a question word |
| `Ju kan dar … ?` | yes/no question in SVO order |
| `Je haf hunger i durst` | *to have hunger*, as in German, Dutch and the Romance languages |
| `gold moneta` | compound, with the modifier first |
| `Rester hir i instruer me.` | imperative: the base form |
| `un gast ke …` | relative clause with `ke` |
